# In-model methods for tracking objects over time and space in video-language models

Scope: methods that change the model's architecture, inputs or training (not external tool wrappers). Research date 2026-11-19. Every number below was read on the cited page during this session; where a page did not show a value it is listed under Gaps. Numbers from different papers use different training data and are not directly comparable unless they come from the same table.

## 1. 3D-aware and spatially grounded VLMs: what they add and how much they gain on VSI-Bench

### Takeaway
The dominant 2025-2026 recipe is a frozen feed-forward geometry encoder (VGGT or CUT3R) fused into the visual tokens plus spatial instruction tuning; it lifts VSI-Bench from about 30-33 (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B zero-shot) to 48-72. But the same-data ablations show that most of the gain comes from the spatial fine-tuning data, not the geometry branch (GeoAlign: 66.8 without geometry vs 71.4 with; Spatial-MLLM: 40.0 vs 46.1), and none of these papers tests objects that move out of view.

### Cited Findings
- **Spatial-MLLM** (arXiv 29 May 2025, v2 19 May 2026). Dual encoder: frozen 2D encoder for semantics plus a frozen spatial encoder initialised from the VGGT backbone; a light connector (two MLPs, summed) merges them into unified visual tokens; connector and LLM backbone are trained; space-aware frame sampling picks 16 frames from 128 candidates at inference. Built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, about 4B parameters total (appendix says 4.2B). Training: SFT then GRPO (RL limited to 1,000 steps for compute reasons); data from ScanQA train, SQA3D train and about 70k self-made QA pairs on ScanNet. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23747), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1)
- Spatial-MLLM-4B scores 48.4 average on VSI-Bench vs 30.6 for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline and 45.4 for Gemini-1.5 Pro. With the same data and 16 frames, Qwen2.5-VL-3B fine-tuned without the spatial branch gets 40.0 and Spatial-MLLM SFT gets 46.1 (so about 9.4 points come from data, about 6.1 from the geometry branch). Training compute is not reported. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1)
- **VLM-3R** (arXiv 26 May 2025, v5 21 Apr 2026). Adds a geometry encoder that produces implicit 3D tokens from monocular video, a "Spatial-Visual-View Fusion" step, and over 200K 3D reconstructive instruction-tuning QA pairs; also introduces the VSTI benchmark (over 138.6K QA pairs, five tasks on changing spatial relationships over time). Project page: https://vlm-3r.github.io/ — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20279)
- VLM-3R uses CUT3R as geometry encoder; in its ablation CUT3R gives the best overall score (60.9), and the authors say VGGT's permutation-equivariant design and reliance on relative-scale priors make it less suited to sequence-aware reasoning and metric-scale judgments (as reported in a search summary of the paper; not read on the paper page in this session, treat the rationale as lightly verified). VLM-3R-7B = 60.9 on VSI-Bench is confirmed in two independent comparison tables. — [GeoAlign table](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1), [SpaceMind table](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23075v2)
- **VG-LLM** ("Learning from Videos for 3D World: Enhancing MLLMs with 3D Vision Geometry Priors", arXiv 30 May 2025, NeurIPS 2025). A 3D visual geometry encoder extracts 3D priors from video and these are combined with visual tokens before the MLLM; no explicit 3D inputs. Abstract claims the 4B model surpasses Gemini-1.5-Pro on VSI-Bench. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24625)
- VG-LLM VSI-Bench averages as listed by a later paper: VG-LLM-4B 47.3, VG-LLM-8B 50.7. The same paper describes the VG-LLM paradigm as adding VGGT features from single layers to the original visual tokens and fine-tuning the MLLM. — [GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1)
- **Cambrian-S** (arXiv 6 Nov 2025, ICLR 2026 poster). Four-stage training (vision-language alignment, image instruction tuning, general video instruction tuning, spatial video instruction tuning) on VSI-590K: 590,667 QA pairs from 5,963 videos and 44,858 images across 10 sources. No geometry encoder; gains come from data. VSI-Bench: 7B 67.5, 3B 57.3, 1.5B 54.8, 0.5B 50.6. Code: https://github.com/cambrian-mllm/cambrian-s — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1), [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007167)
- **SpaceMind** (arXiv 2511.23075, v2 4 Dec 2025). RGB-only; frozen InternViT-300M plus frozen VGGT spatial encoder; a Camera-Guided Modality Fusion module (camera-conditioned bias on spatial keys/values, query-independent importance weights, camera-conditioned gate) before the LLM; LoRA rank 256 on InternVL3-8B. Trained on VLM-3R data, ViCA-322K and SQA3D train, 2 epochs, 32 frames, about 25 hours on 64 H100 GPUs. VSI-Bench 69.6. Code promised, no link on the page. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23075v2)
- **GeoAlign** (arXiv 14 Apr 2026). Builds a feature bank from several intermediate VGGT layers; the MLLM's own visual tokens query a light router that picks the top-2 layers per patch; fused features are added residually to the visual stream. Base Qwen2.5-VL-3B (model called GeoAlign-4B); ViT and VGGT frozen, fusion module and LLM trained; about 460K samples, 1 epoch, batch 64, lr 1e-5, 8 H800 GPUs. VSI-Bench 71.4. Its "2D-Only" ablation (Qwen2.5-VL-3B fine-tuned on the same data without geometry) scores 66.8. No code link on the page. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1)
- Reference zero-shot VSI-Bench averages from the same table: Qwen2.5-VL-7B 33.0, Qwen2.5-VL-72B 37.0, GPT-4o 34.0, Gemini-1.5-Pro 45.4, Gemini-2.5-Pro 53.6, InternVL3-78B 48.4, Cambrian-S-3B 57.3. — [GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1)
- GeoThinker is reported at a peak of 72.6 on VSI-Bench (search-result summary only; paper page not opened). — [arXiv 2602.06037](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06037)
- A 2026 paper, "Multi-View Relational Distillation for Spatial Reasoning with Vision-Language Models", distils geometry features into the VLM instead of attaching the encoder and compares against plain supervised fine-tuning on VSI-Bench (search-result summary only; numbers not read). — [arXiv 2608.10864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10864)

### Inferences
- For a 3B model the cheapest verified gain is spatial instruction tuning alone: a plain fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B reaches 40.0 (Spatial-MLLM data) or 66.8 (GeoAlign's 460K data) on VSI-Bench. The geometry branch adds 4.6-6.1 points on top. This is the same base model as the project.
- Feasibility on one 24 GB GPU: attaching frozen VGGT (about 1B extra parameters, per the 3B to 4B jump) with a trained MLP connector and LoRA is plausible at 16 frames, but every published run used multi-GPU clusters (8 H800, 64 H100) and 120K-590K samples; a few-week replication would have to be a small-data version. This is an estimate, not a reported result.
- These models address static-scene layout (VSI-Bench is built on indoor scans). Geometry features describe what is visible; they do not by themselves carry an object that has left the view. VLM-3R's CUT3R argument (sequence-aware, metric scale) is the closest to the project's need, since CUT3R keeps a recurrent state.
- The project's failure with "state as visual-stream tokens" is a caution here: the geometry-fusion papers all train the LLM (full or high-rank LoRA 256) on hundreds of thousands of samples so that it learns to read the new tokens; a low-rank LoRA with little data may again ignore them.

### Gaps
- SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, LLaVA-3D, Video-3D LLM, GPT4Scene: not opened in this session; no verified numbers. From background knowledge only (unverified): SpatialVLM trains on synthetic metric spatial QA; SpatialRGPT adds region and depth inputs; LLaVA-3D and Video-3D LLM add 3D position embeddings to patch tokens using known depth and camera poses; GPT4Scene adds a bird's-eye-view image and object markers. They did not appear in the VSI-Bench tables read here.
- VLM-3R base model, exact training compute and code repository URL were not shown on the abstract page.
- No paper in this family reports an out-of-view or occlusion-persistence test.
- Whether GeoAlign trains the LLM fully or with LoRA is not stated on the page.

## 2. Memory and state inside the model: streaming memory, predictive sensing, world-model objectives

### Takeaway
Streaming video LLMs mostly manage which visual tokens or KV-cache entries are kept; they are memory-management systems, not object-state trackers. The one in-model objective aimed at "expectation" is Cambrian-S's latent frame prediction head, whose prediction error (surprise) drives memory compression and event segmentation; it is evaluated on long-horizon recall and counting, not on invisible moving objects. 2026 benchmarks confirm that object persistence under occlusion remains unsolved for video LLMs.

### Cited Findings
- **Cambrian-S predictive sensing** (Nov 2025). A latent frame prediction (LFP) head, a two-layer MLP running in parallel with the language head, predicts the latent representation of the next video frame; losses are MSE and cosine distance to the actual next-frame features, weighted against the next-token loss; the SigLIP encoder is frozen; 1 FPS. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- Surprise use in Cambrian-S: each incoming frame's surprise is attached to its KV cache; frames below a threshold are compressed 2x before entering a fixed-size long-term memory; when full, the least surprising frames are dropped or merged; at query time the top-K frames are retrieved by cosine similarity to the query. For counting, frame features are buffered until a high-surprise frame, the buffer is summarised into a segment answer, and segment answers are aggregated. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- Cambrian-S results on VSI-SUPER: without memory, Cambrian-S-7B VSR is 38.3 (10 min), 35.0 (30 min), 6.0 (60 min), 0.0 (120 and 240 min). Gemini-2.5-Flash: VSR 41.5 and VSC 10.9 at 60 min, out of context at 120 min. With surprise segmentation, Cambrian-S VSC is about 38% MRA at 10 min and about 28% at 120 min, while Gemini-Live and GPT-Realtime are under 15% at 10 min and near zero at 120 min. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- A follow-up, "Towards Spatial Supersensing in the Wild" (arXiv 2607.13681), introduces VSI-SUPER-WILD and reports 13 mainstream MLLMs performing weakly, often near chance (search-result summary; paper not opened). — [arXiv 2607.13681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13681)
- **Flash-VStream**: fixed-size "Flash Memory" with a learnable STAR memory (Spatial-Temporal-Abstract-Retrieved) that keeps informative frames and reduces temporal redundancy. — [arXiv 2406.08085](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08085v1), [arXiv 2506.23825](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23825v2)
- **StreamBridge** (NeurIPS 2025): converts offline Video-LLMs to streaming and attaches a separate activation model for proactive responses; it does not add an object-level memory. — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bf6939f9058a391c47014731b2486e2a-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Other streaming-memory work found (search summaries only): StreamMem (Meta/NYU, 2025), ReKV (stores the full KV cache, offloaded to RAM or disk), FluxMem (Mar 2026, adaptive hierarchical memory), LiveStarPro (Jun 2026). — [StreamMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15717v1), [FluxMem](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02096v1), [LiveStarPro](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17798)
- **TOC-Bench** (arXiv 11 May 2026): diagnostic benchmark for temporal object consistency (occlusion, disappearance, reappearance, state transitions); each subject tied to a per-frame trajectory; 2,323 human-verified QA pairs over 1,951 videos; a temporal-necessity filter removed 60.7% of candidates. Finds weaknesses in event counting, ordering and identity-sensitive reasoning even for models strong on general video benchmarks. No method proposed. Code: https://github.com/cjzcjz666/toc_bench — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09904)
- **StateTrace / HSR-Bench** (arXiv 19 Aug 2026): targets "hidden-state" reasoning (objects hidden, occluded or contained). The method is an external pipeline (grounding module plus SAM2 propagation, a graph memory of trajectories and occlusion/containment events, retrieved and written as a semantic summary plus a spatial timeline that the VideoLLM reads); model weights are unchanged. HSR-Bench: 1,427 QA over 1,384 videos built from OVIS. Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 29.85 to 57.74; VideoLLaMA3: 39.6 to 64.2; heavy occlusion VideoLLaMA3-7B 37.28% to 63.34%. Removing the spatial summary caused the largest ablation drop. No code link on the page. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18532)
- Adjacent, for video generation models rather than video LLMs: MemoBench (arXiv 2606.27537) reports no current model reliably keeps object memory across occlusion; "Training Object Permanence in World Models" (arXiv 2609.28654) releases a 1.5M-sample corpus (search summaries only). — [MemoBench](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.27537.md), [arXiv 2609.28654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28654)

### Inferences
- StateTrace is independent confirmation, on the project's exact base model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B), that written state timelines are what a frozen video LLM can use: 29.85 to 57.74. It is the same tool-outside design the project already has, so it supports the baseline but does not answer the professor's request.
- Cambrian-S's LFP head is the most concrete in-model "world model" objective and is cheap to add (two-layer MLP, MSE plus cosine on frozen-encoder features). It trains the backbone to carry forward-predictive information. However, its measured benefit is through memory management, not through better answers about unseen objects; no result shows that the LFP loss alone improves object-persistence QA. It also resembles the project's failed auxiliary trajectory loss in being an auxiliary objective whose features the answer head may not use.
- No streaming-memory paper found here targets persistence of objects that are no longer visible; they target which frames to keep.

### Gaps
- No verified source found for recurrent or slot-based object memories inside a video LLM, or for "spatial memory tokens"; the single search on this did not surface any. I found no reliable sources on this.
- JEPA-style objectives used to train a VLM (as opposed to a vision encoder): not searched specifically; no verified finding.
- VideoLLM-online was described only in a search summary (streaming EOS prediction, no explicit memory bank); not opened.
- LFP loss weight and surprise thresholds are not given in the Cambrian-S text read. Training compute not stated (acknowledges Google TPU Research Cloud).
- TOC-Bench per-model scores were not on the abstract page.

## 3. Training the model to output or reason through explicit state (coordinates, tracks, chain-of-thought)

### Takeaway
The strongest and most directly relevant evidence in this whole survey is "Can Vision-Language Models Solve the Shell Game?" (Mar 2026): fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-3B to answer directly stayed at chance after 60 epochs, while having the model write object trajectories as text before answering (Spatiotemporal Grounded Chain-of-Thought, SGCoT) reached 91% with no external tools, and the paper gives a complexity argument for why a fixed-depth transformer needs the written intermediate state.

### Cited Findings
- **VET-Bench / SGCoT** (Tiedong Liu, Wee Sun Lee; arXiv 9 Mar 2026). VET-Bench is a synthetic shell-game benchmark with visually identical objects, trackable only through spatiotemporal continuity. Reported accuracies near the 0.33 chance level for 3 objects: Gemini-3-Pro 0.34, Gemini-3-Flash 0.30, Qwen3-VL-8B 0.30, GLM-4.6V-Flash 0.34, Doubao-Seed-2.0 0.30. Code and data: https://vetbench.github.io — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08436), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- SGCoT format: the model writes `<tracks coords="timestamp object_idx x y;...">Object</tracks>` with timestamps every 0.5 s and x, y normalised to 0-1000, then a short answer ("Answer: left"); the final coordinate determines the answer. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- SGCoT training: Molmo2 (which already has native video tracking output) is fine-tuned on about 300 synthesised text-only samples with no video input; loss is masked on the trajectory tokens so only the final answer is supervised; one epoch takes about 3 minutes on a single A100; accuracy rises from near chance to 91%. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- Direct-answer ablation on the project's base model: Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct trained on 500 synthesised cups-game videos at 8 FPS; the loss stayed at chance level even after 60 epochs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- Theory: visual entity tracking with k >= 5 objects is NC1-complete; assuming TC0 is strictly inside NC1, fixed-depth transformers cannot solve it without intermediate computation (chain of thought). For k = 2 it reduces to parity. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- Limits stated by the authors: VET-Bench does not test occlusion or out-of-view objects; real tracking with occlusion, overlap and motion blur "may require stronger physical priors or world models"; errors come from identity switches that show up as abrupt jumps in the written trajectory. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- **Molmo2** (Ai2; arXiv 2601.10611, Jan 2026; CVPR 2026 oral): open weights, data and training code; sizes Molmo2-4B, Molmo2-8B, Molmo2-O-7B; supports video pointing and tracking as text output. Reported video pointing F1 38.4 vs 20.0 for Gemini 3 Pro in the v1 abstract; a CVPR listing gives different figures (32.9% vs 17% for Gemini 2.5 Pro), so the versions conflict. Code: https://github.com/allenai/molmo2 — [arXiv 2601.10611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10611), [CVPR listing](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/oral/40278), [Ai2 blog](https://allenai.org/blog/molmo2)
- **Elysium** (ByteDance, ECCV 2024): end-to-end MLLM that outputs object boxes per frame as text for single-object tracking, referring SOT and video referring expression generation; trained on ElysiumTrack-1M (1.27 million annotated video frames with boxes and descriptions); multi-object tracking left to future work. — [arXiv 2403.16558](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16558v2)
- **Sa2VA** (ByteDance, Jan 2025): the LLM emits instruction tokens that prompt SAM-2 to produce masks; tracking output is masks from the SAM-2 decoder, not coordinates in text. Sa2VA and VideoGLaMM both use SAM2 without fine-tuning its memory components; Sa2VA-i (Sep 2025) fixes a train/inference mismatch. — [Sa2VA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04001), [Sa2VA-i](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19082v2)
- Visual-trace work from robotics (search summaries only): TraceVLA overlays multi-point motion paths on the image as a visual prompt and fine-tunes OpenVLA on about 150K robot trajectories; CoT-VLA predicts future frames as visual goals before acting. — [TraceVLA](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10345), [CoT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22020v1)

### Inferences
- SGCoT matches the project's own evidence: the model reads state written as a sentence at 81% but ignores the same state as latent tokens (5-10%). SGCoT keeps the state in the text channel but makes the model itself write it, which is "in-model" in the sense the professor asked for (no tracker, no calculator at inference).
- The 300-sample text-only recipe worked because Molmo2 could already emit tracks. Qwen2.5-VL-3B cannot, so the project would need to supervise the track tokens too (the project already has per-frame floor positions from its simulator to use as labels). The fact that the project's small probe network recovers floor positions from the frozen vision tokens shows the information is present in the LM's input.
- VET-Bench stops where the project starts: after the object leaves view, the model must continue the written track by extrapolation. That adds an arithmetic step (constant-velocity continuation) to the scratchpad. No verified result exists for that step, so it is the novel part of a project experiment.
- The complexity argument gives the write-up a principled explanation for the earlier failures of direct-answer training and of single-forward-pass tricks (rotary shifts, auxiliary losses).
- Sa2VA-style designs move tracking into SAM-2's memory, which is an attached module rather than the language model, and SAM-2 does not track objects outside the frame; less relevant.

### Gaps
- Kosmos-2, Shikra, Qwen-VL box output, Artemis, VideoGLaMM details, "TrackGPT": not opened; no verified facts. From background knowledge only (unverified): Kosmos-2 and Shikra showed LLMs can emit box coordinates as text/location tokens; Qwen2.5-VL natively outputs absolute-pixel boxes and points in JSON, which suggests the 3B model already has a coordinate-writing prior to build on.
- SGCoT appendix details (which Molmo2 size, LoRA or full fine-tuning) were truncated on the page.
- No paper found that supervises a written track through an occlusion or out-of-view interval and evaluates the post-disappearance position.

## 4. Distilling a tool or algorithm into the model; latent reasoning tokens

### Takeaway
Latent-reasoning methods (Coconut, Mirage, LVR, CoVT) replace written intermediate steps with continuous tokens and report gains on static-image perception benchmarks, typically a few points to 16%; all need staged training in which the latent tokens are first supervised against a concrete target. None is shown on video tracking, and the project has already seen its model ignore latent state tokens, so these are higher risk than written state.

### Cited Findings
- **Coconut** ("Training Large Language Models to Reason in a Continuous Latent Space", arXiv 9 Dec 2024): the last hidden state is fed back as the next input embedding instead of being decoded to a word; a continuous thought can encode several alternative next steps (breadth-first-like search); outperforms CoT on logical reasoning tasks needing substantial search. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769)
- **Mirage / Machine Mental Imagery** (arXiv 20 Jun 2025; CVPR 2026 poster): interleaves latent visual tokens into text decoding without generating pixels. Training: (1) supervise latent tokens by distilling from ground-truth image embeddings, (2) switch to text-only supervision so the latent trajectory aligns with the task, (3) a reinforcement learning stage. Project page https://vlm-mirage.github.io/ — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17218), [CVPR page](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/39854)
- **LVR / Latent Visual Reasoning** (arXiv 29 Sep 2025): the LM generates latent states that reconstruct question-relevant visual tokens of the input image, interleaved with text; adds a GRPO variant for latent reasoning; 71.67% on MMVP vs 66.67% for Qwen2.5-VL. Code "will be released later" per the page. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24251)
- **CoVT / Chain-of-Visual-Thought** (arXiv 24 Nov 2025, v3 5 Aug 2026): the VLM reasons through about 20 continuous visual tokens that are trained to reconstruct dense signals from lightweight vision experts (depth, segmentation, edges, DINO features); applied to Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA; improves 3% to 16% across more than ten perception benchmarks (CV-Bench, MMVP, RealWorldQA, MMStar, WorldMedQA, HRBench). Project page https://wakalsprojectpage.github.io/covt-website/ — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19418)
- **Perceptio** (NeurIPS 2026 listing): the model emits segmentation and depth tokens in-sequence before its answer, with depth knowledge distilled from a depth-generation teacher (search summary only). — [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/164949)
- **SpatialTraceGen** (NeurIPS 2025): a dataset of multi-tool spatial reasoning traces distilled from a large teacher, intended for fine-tuning and offline RL of VLM tool use (search summary only; the student still calls tools). — [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/126640)
- On why injected continuous tokens get ignored (search summaries of mechanistic papers, not opened): soft prompts differ strongly from normal prompts and there is a modality gap between text and visual representations in VLMs; patching visual token representations from later to earlier layers closes 32% of the gap between analogous visual and textual prompts; arbitrary tokens placed in visual slots can degrade accuracy. — [Towards Interpreting Visual Information Processing in VLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.07149), [Same Task, Different Circuits](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09047), [Bridging the Missing-Modality Gap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.12517)

### Inferences
- The common pattern in Mirage, LVR and CoVT is that the model generates the latent tokens itself and they are supervised against a decodable target (image embeddings, depth/segmentation decoders). The project's failed variant fed externally computed state tokens in as input. A CoVT-style variant for the project would have the model emit a few continuous tokens that a small decoder head must turn into each object's floor position (including after it leaves view), then answer. This is different from what failed, but it carries the same risk that the answer path does not attend to those tokens.
- A practical bridge is Coconut's idea applied in reverse order of risk: first train the written-track scratchpad (section 3), then progressively replace written steps with latent ones. This is my reading of how the method is usually staged, not something verified on the Coconut abstract page.
- The "Same Task, Different Circuits" result (text and visual versions of a task use different circuits, aligned only in later layers) is a plausible explanation of the project's 81% vs 5-10% gap and could be cited as such, with the caveat that it was read only as a search summary.

### Gaps
- No verified source found for distilling a tracker-plus-calculator pipeline into VLM weights so that it answers without tools; the search returned none matching that framing.
- No verified source gathered on teaching arithmetic or extrapolation to LLMs (scratchpads, positional schemes such as Abacus embeddings); not searched in this session. From background knowledge only (unverified): scratchpad-style step-by-step supervision is the standard remedy for multi-step arithmetic in transformers.
- Coconut, Mirage and LVR base-model sizes, compute and benchmark numbers were not on the abstract pages (except LVR's MMVP figure). CoVT and Mirage code repository URLs not confirmed.
- No latent-reasoning paper found that evaluates on video or on objects out of view.

## 5. Per family: most relevant result, benchmark, model size, data/compute, code

### Takeaway
A compact table of the verified headline for each family; the only results on the project's own base model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) are Spatial-MLLM/GeoAlign (geometry fusion), the VET-Bench direct-answer failure, and StateTrace (external).

### Cited Findings
- Geometry-encoder fusion: GeoAlign-4B on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, VSI-Bench 71.4 (66.8 for the same fine-tune without geometry), about 460K samples, 8 H800 GPUs, no code link seen. — [GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1)
- Geometry-encoder fusion, earlier: Spatial-MLLM-4B on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, VSI-Bench 48.4 vs 30.6 baseline; frozen VGGT; 16 frames; compute not reported. — [Spatial-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1)
- Geometry plus camera conditioning: SpaceMind on InternVL3-8B, VSI-Bench 69.6, LoRA rank 256, 64 H100 for about 25 h. — [SpaceMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23075v2)
- Recurrent geometry encoder: VLM-3R-7B, VSI-Bench 60.9, over 200K QA pairs; also a temporal benchmark VSTI. — [VLM-3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20279), [GeoAlign table](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1)
- Data-only spatial tuning: Cambrian-S-3B, VSI-Bench 57.3 with VSI-590K; open code. — [Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- Predictive objective plus surprise memory: Cambrian-S, VSI-SUPER counting about 38% MRA at 10 min vs under 15% for Gemini-Live/GPT-Realtime; two-layer MLP head. — [Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- Written-track chain of thought: Molmo2-SGCoT, VET-Bench 91% vs chance 33%; about 300 text-only samples, about 3 minutes per epoch on one A100; code and data at vetbench.github.io. — [Shell Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- Direct-answer fine-tuning (negative result): Qwen2.5-VL-3B, 500 videos, 60 epochs, loss at chance. — [Shell Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- Coordinate-output tracking LLM: Elysium, 1.27M annotated frames. — [Elysium](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16558v2)
- Latent visual reasoning: CoVT, about 20 continuous tokens, 3-16% gains on image perception benchmarks, on Qwen2.5-VL. LVR, MMVP 71.67 vs 66.67. — [CoVT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19418), [LVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24251)
- External state memory (for contrast): StateTrace, HSR-Bench Qwen2.5-VL-3B 29.85 to 57.74. — [StateTrace](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.18532)

### Inferences
- Feasibility ranking for 3B + LoRA + one 24 GB GPU + a few weeks (my estimate): written-track scratchpad (very cheap, text supervision only) > LFP auxiliary head (cheap to add, uncertain payoff) > CoVT-style self-generated latent state tokens with a decoder head (moderate, risky) > frozen geometry encoder fusion (heaviest: extra 1B-parameter encoder in memory, needs large data to make the LLM read the new tokens).

### Gaps
- Code availability could not be confirmed for Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM, GeoAlign, SpaceMind, LVR, CoVT, Mirage beyond project pages; several likely have repositories that were simply not on the pages read.
- Model sizes for Mirage, LVR, Coconut not verified.

## 6. The 3-4 most promising approaches for this project and a minimal experiment for each

### Takeaway
Given that the model reads written state but ignores latent state, and that direct-answer training fails on the same base model in published work, the best first in-model step is to make the LoRA-tuned model write the track and its extrapolation itself (SGCoT extended past the frame edge), then optionally compress that scratchpad into supervised latent tokens; geometry fusion and latent-frame prediction are secondary options with weaker evidence for this specific failure.

### Cited Findings
- Evidence for approach A (self-written track): SGCoT 91% vs chance on VET-Bench without tools; direct-answer Qwen2.5-VL-3B stuck at chance; NC1-completeness argument for needing intermediate computation. — [Shell Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)
- Evidence for approach B (self-generated, decoder-supervised latent tokens): CoVT's about 20 continuous tokens trained to reconstruct expert outputs give 3-16% on perception benchmarks on Qwen2.5-VL; Mirage's two-stage schedule (latent supervision first, then text-only). — [CoVT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19418), [Mirage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17218)
- Evidence for approach C (predictive auxiliary head): Cambrian-S LFP head (two-layer MLP, MSE plus cosine on next-frame features, frozen encoder). — [Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- Evidence for approach D (geometry encoder): +6.1 (Spatial-MLLM) and +4.6 (GeoAlign) VSI-Bench points over same-data fine-tunes of Qwen2.5-VL-3B; VLM-3R's preference for the recurrent CUT3R over VGGT for sequence-aware, metric-scale reasoning. — [Spatial-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1), [GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1), [VLM-3R summary via search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20279)

### Inferences
All experiment designs below are my proposals, not published protocols.

- **A. Self-written track and extrapolation (SGCoT extended out of view). Highest priority.**
  - Change: training targets only. The LoRA model's answer becomes: per-object track lines (time, object id, floor x, y at fixed intervals while visible), then one or two explicit extrapolation lines for the time after exit (velocity estimate, predicted position at query time), then the answer. No tracker, calculator or state sentence at inference.
  - Minimal experiment: reuse the existing simulator labels to generate the target text; train LoRA with full loss on track, extrapolation and answer tokens. Report (i) track error while visible, (ii) extrapolated position error, (iii) final answer accuracy, against the 81% written-state pipeline and the direct-answer baseline. Ablate: answer only; track only then answer; track plus extrapolation then answer. Use the counterfactual twin videos as a test set: a shortcut cannot produce the right written track.
  - Why it fits the failures: it stays in the text channel that the model demonstrably reads; the supervision is dense per token, unlike the auxiliary trajectory loss whose features the answer path could ignore.
  - Risks: 64 tokens per 224x224 frame may limit position precision; multi-digit extrapolation arithmetic in text may be the weak step (use coarse grid units and a fixed step format); output length grows with objects and frames.
  - Cost: text-only target changes, same LoRA setup; the published analogue trained in minutes, though that relied on Molmo2's existing tracking skill, so expect substantially more training here.
- **B. Compress the scratchpad into self-generated latent state tokens with a decoder head (CoVT/Mirage style). Second stage after A works.**
  - Change: add K special tokens per object that the model emits before answering; their last-layer hidden states feed a small MLP that must regress the object's position at query time (and optionally velocity); stage 1 trains with regression plus answer loss, stage 2 drops the regression loss.
  - Minimal experiment: K = 4-8 tokens; compare answer accuracy and decoded-position error with A; run an intervention test (swap the latent tokens between two videos and check whether the answer follows) to verify the model actually uses them, which is the failure seen before.
  - Difference from what failed: tokens are produced by the model and sit in its own output stream with a decodable target, instead of being injected as foreign input embeddings.
  - Risk: same ignore-the-tokens failure; evidence so far is from static images only.
- **C. Latent frame prediction auxiliary head (Cambrian-S style). Cheap side experiment.**
  - Change: a two-layer MLP on the LM hidden states at each frame's last token predicts the next frame's frozen vision features (MSE plus cosine), trained jointly with the answer loss.
  - Minimal experiment: add to the direct-answer and to approach-A training; probe whether the object's post-exit position becomes linearly decodable from LM hidden states at later frames; check answer accuracy change.
  - Risk: once the ball is out of view, next-frame features contain no ball, so the objective does not directly reward remembering it; likely helps motion encoding while visible only. Similar in spirit to the earlier auxiliary loss that did not help.
- **D. Frozen geometry encoder fused into visual tokens (Spatial-MLLM/GeoAlign recipe; CUT3R if sequence state is wanted). Lowest priority within the time budget.**
  - Change: run frozen VGGT or CUT3R on the frames, map features to the 64-token grid with an MLP, add to the visual tokens; train connector plus LoRA.
  - Minimal experiment: compare with and without the branch on the project task and inside approach A (does it improve the written track's metric accuracy?).
  - Risk: gains in the literature required 120K-460K samples and LLM training beyond low-rank LoRA; the branch improves visible-scene geometry, not memory of departed objects; memory pressure on 24 GB.
- Suggested framing for the report: published work on the same 3B model shows direct-answer training fails on tracking (VET-Bench) and that written state helps (StateTrace, SGCoT); the project's in-model contribution would be showing whether a small VLM can produce and extrapolate that state itself for out-of-view objects, a case the SGCoT authors explicitly leave open.

### Gaps
- No published result exists (that I found) for any of A-D on out-of-view extrapolation; all expected outcomes are extrapolations from neighbouring tasks.
- Whether Qwen2.5-VL-3B at 64 tokens per frame can write metric floor coordinates accurately is untested in the literature read; the project's own probe network is the only evidence that the information is present.
- Molmo2-4B (native tracking, open training code) could be an alternative base model for approach A, but its memory footprint under LoRA on 24 GB and its behaviour on the project's videos are unknown.

## 7. Distilling a harness / agent / tool pipeline into the model

### Takeaway
The established recipe is Visual Program Distillation (CVPR 2024): run the tool pipeline, keep only traces whose final answer is correct, rewrite the trace as natural-language step-by-step reasoning, and fine-tune the VLM with LoRA to produce reasoning plus answer in one pass; the distilled model ended up more accurate than the programs it was distilled from. Self-training variants (R3V, STL) show that using the model's own correct and incorrect traces beats both plain STaR filtering and distillation from a larger teacher.

### Cited Findings
- **Visual Program Distillation (VPD)** (Hu et al., Google Research / UW; arXiv Dec 2023, CVPR 2024). An LLM writes k=5 candidate programs (temperature 0.5) that call vision tools; programs that fail to execute are dropped; with labels, a program is kept only if its output matches the label; the execution trace is rewritten by an LLM (PaLM-2, 20 few-shot examples) into a natural-language rationale; the VLM is fine-tuned to output rationale and answer. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03052v1), [CVPR page](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2024/poster/30913)
- VPD training details: multitask LoRA (rank 8) on PaLI-3 (5B) and PaLI-X (55B); loss is the sum of a label term and a rationale term, each length-normalised; batch 128, 8,000 steps; about 310.5K labelled examples of which 89.6K have synthesised chains of thought; PaLI-X-VPD took about 2 days on 128 TPU-v3. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03052v1)
- VPD results: sampling 5 programs instead of 1 raised the fraction of examples with a correct program by +45% on GQA and A-OKVQA, +33% on OK-VQA, +10% on TallyQA. PaLI-X-VPD vs the same model instruction-tuned without VPD: GQA +1.6, TallyQA complex +1.2, MMBench +1.2, better on 11 of 12 tasks. The distilled model has "much higher accuracy than visual programs on all tasks" (numbers only in a figure). Without labels (Hateful Memes, top-1 executable program, no filtering): 70.8% accuracy. Project page: visual-program-distillation.github.io; code release not confirmed. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03052v1)
- **R3V** ("Vision-Language Models Can Self-Improve Reasoning via Reflection", arXiv 30 Oct 2024). Iteratively samples 3 chain-of-thought solutions per question from the model itself, labels them positive or negative by answer correctness, and fine-tunes with SFT on positives plus a self-refine loss (correct a flawed rationale) and a self-select loss (choose the right answer among candidate rationales); 4-5 iterations, LoRA, 3 epochs per iteration. Average over benchmarks, Qwen-VL: GPT-distilled 48.47, STaR 59.28, R3V 64.37; LLaVA-1.5: 45.24, 55.81, 59.03. Code: https://github.com/njucckevin/MM-Self-Improve — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00855v1)
- **See, Think, Learn (STL)** (WACV 2026, arXiv 2512.02456): self-training loop where the VLM generates positive rationales and negative rationales (why wrong choices fail) and retrains on both; reported to beat training on answers only or on self-generated reasoning only (search summary; paper not opened). — [arXiv 2512.02456](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02456)
- **Back-translated tool traces** (Huang et al., arXiv 23 Jun 2025, ICML 2025 workshop): a solver agent interleaves planning, symbolic tool calls and reflection; a translator agent explains each tool call and a rephrase agent merges them into one coherent narrative; a small open model is fine-tuned on these so it needs no tools at inference; gains on competition maths (no numbers on the abstract page). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19171)
- **Guava** (arXiv 16 Jun 2026): distils a frontier-VLM tool-using manipulation harness into a 4B open model using fewer than 2K trajectories collected entirely in simulation; abstract claims performance comparable to frontier proprietary models. A search summary adds that trajectories are validated in simulation and that SFT is followed by RL for long-horizon tasks (2,268 trajectories), which the abstract page did not confirm. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18363)
- Text-only agent distillation, for reference (search summaries only): Agent Distillation (arXiv 2505.17612; student still uses tools); pi-Distill (arXiv 2602.04942; student trained to act without privileged information the teacher had); Agentic-R1 (EMNLP 2025). LiteMedCoT-VL (arXiv 2605.09384) transfers chain of thought from a 235B teacher to 2B students with LoRA, 48.7% to 64.9% on PMC-VQA. — [Agent Distillation](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17612v2), [pi-Distill](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04942v3), [LiteMedCoT-VL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09384)
- Teacher = algorithm, closest verified case: SGCoT, where trajectory text in a fixed format is synthesised by code (about 300 text-only samples) and the model learns to answer from its own written tracks, 91% on VET-Bench. — [Shell Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436)

### Inferences
- The project's harness maps onto VPD almost one to one: tools = position estimator and calculator; trace = the written track record and extrapolation; filter = keep traces whose final answer is correct (the simulator gives this for free, and also gives twin-pair correctness as a stricter filter); student target = the trace as text followed by the answer. VPD's finding that the student beats the pipeline is expected to transfer because filtering removes the teacher's 25-33% wrong traces.
- The project has a better teacher than its own harness: the simulator's exact positions. Three trace sources are possible and worth comparing: (a) harness traces, correct ones only (pure self-distillation, on the harness's distribution, works without ground truth and so would transfer to real video); (b) oracle traces written from simulator ground truth (100% correct, unlimited); (c) a mix. This comparison is my proposal, not a published result.
- Off-policy vs on-policy: VPD and (a)/(b) are off-policy (student trained on teacher traces under teacher forcing). The known weakness is compounding error at inference, when the student conditions on its own imperfect earlier track lines. R3V-style iterations are the on-policy counterpart: sample the student's own full outputs, keep correct ones, and train a "refine" task on incorrect ones. With a simulator, a stronger on-policy variant is available: let the student write its track, then relabel every line with the true positions and train on the corrected continuation. I found no VLM paper that does exactly this relabelling.
- Minimal experiment (distillation): generate 5-20K simulator videos; produce trace text in one fixed format (per-frame floor positions while visible, velocity line, extrapolated position, answer); LoRA-train with a VPD-style two-term loss (answer term plus rationale term, each length-normalised). Conditions: answer-only (expected about chance, per VET-Bench); harness-correct traces; oracle traces. Then 2-3 R3V-style rounds on the student's own outputs. Report accuracy on held-out videos and on twin pairs (both twins correct), plus position error of the written track against ground truth.
- Trace format matters: back-translation and VPD both rewrite tool traces into fluent natural language, while SGCoT uses a terse coordinate format. For a 3B model and long tracks, the terse format is cheaper; this is an open design choice to ablate.

### Gaps
- No paper found that distils an algorithmic tracker or state estimator specifically into a VLM for video; SGCoT is the nearest (and relies on Molmo2 already tracking).
- No verified source for on-policy (DAgger-style) relabelling of VLM reasoning traces.
- STaR itself (Zelikman et al., 2022) was not opened; it appears only as a baseline inside R3V.
- VPD code availability not confirmed; Guava and back-translation code links not found; back-translation student size and numbers not on the abstract page.

## 8. Reinforcement learning for VLMs (GRPO-style) with answer-correctness reward

### Takeaway
R1-style RL for video and spatial reasoning always follows a supervised warm start and adds a modest gain on top of it: SpaceR +4.0 VSI-Bench points over SFT on the same data (41.6 to 45.6), Video-R1 +2.3 over SFT-only (34.8 to 37.1), both on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with 4-8 80 GB GPUs. Rewards are answer correctness plus format, with shaped extras; Video-R1's contrastive reward (ordered vs shuffled frames) is a direct precedent for using the project's twin-pair signal as a reward.

### Cited Findings
- **Video-R1** (Feng et al., arXiv 2503.21776). Base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Stage 1: SFT cold start, one epoch on Video-R1-CoT-165k (about 40 hours). Stage 2: RL on Video-R1-260k for 1k steps (about 15 hours); up to 8 A100 80 GB; up to 16 frames at 128x28x28 pixels in training; lr 1e-6, KL beta 0.04. Code: https://github.com/tulerfeng/Video-R1 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21776v4)
- Video-R1 reward (T-GRPO): for each question, one group of G=8 responses on ordered frames and one group of G/2 on shuffled frames; if the fraction correct on ordered frames is at least that on shuffled frames, correct ordered-group responses get a bonus of 0.3; base reward is correctness (binary for multiple choice and exact numbers, relative error for regression) plus a format reward; a length bonus of 0.2 for correct responses of 320-512 tokens. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21776v4)
- Video-R1 results at 64 frames (base with CoT / SFT-only / Video-R1): VSI-Bench 31.4 / 34.8 / 37.1; VideoMMMU 50.4 / 49.4 / 52.4; MVBench 59.2 / 60.6 / 64.8. The temporal reward adds about +1.9 VSI-Bench, +1.5 VideoMMMU, +1.6 MVBench over plain GRPO (16-frame ablation). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21776v4)
- **SpaceR** (Ouyang et al., arXiv 2504.01805, v2 21 May 2025). Base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct; SpaceR-151k = 91k spatial QA from ScanNet plus 60k general samples from Video-R1-260k; 8 L20 or 4 A800 GPUs, up to 2 epochs, 8 sampled responses per prompt. Rewards: format (think/answer tags), binary multiple-choice match, numerical relative accuracy averaged over thresholds 0.5-0.95, a map reward (distance between predicted and true object positions on an MxM grid that the model writes as "map imagination", granted only when the task answer is correct), and a length reward. Code: https://github.com/OuyangKun10/SpaceR — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01805v2)
- SpaceR results on VSI-Bench: base 34.4, SFT on the same data 41.6, SG-RLVR 45.6. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01805v2)
- **Spatial-MLLM** also follows SFT with GRPO (1,000 RL steps, limited by compute): SFT-only 46.1 vs final 48.4 on VSI-Bench. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1)
- **ViGoRL** ("Grounded Reinforcement Learning for Visual Reasoning", arXiv 29 May 2025, v3 15 May 2026): RL-trained VLM that anchors each reasoning step to image coordinates, with a multi-turn variant that zooms into predicted coordinates; 86.4% on V*Bench; reported to beat SFT and conventional RL baselines on SAT-2, BLINK, V*Bench, ScreenSpot, VisualWebArena. Project page https://visually-grounded-rl.github.io/ — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23678)
- Small-scale feasibility evidence (search summaries only, papers not opened): a GRPO config on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with LoRA rank 16 / alpha 32 on q_proj and v_proj, 8 generations per prompt, 256-token completions (arXiv 2606.15651); a Stanford CS224R course project doing GRPO plus LoRA on Qwen2.5-VL-3B; a chart-reasoning paper reporting GRPO with LoRA on a single GPU lifting Qwen3-VL-4B to 0.634 on ChartQAPro vs 0.580 for the untuned 8B model (arXiv 2604.03157); a chest X-ray study using 2,000 SFT and 1,000 RL samples on one A100. — [arXiv 2606.15651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15651), [CS224R report](https://cs224r.stanford.edu/spring_2025/projects/pdfs/CS224R_Final_Project_Report-2.pdf), [arXiv 2604.03157](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03157), [arXiv 2512.23090](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23090v1)
- R3V reports that DPO-style preference training failed in the multimodal setting (search summary). — [R3V](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00855v1)

### Inferences
- RL is a second stage, not a first: every verified result starts from an SFT/CoT warm start, and the model alone at 5% would give almost no positive reward to learn from. Order for the project: distil traces (section 7), then GRPO.
- Expected size of the RL gain, from the three same-data comparisons: +2.3 to +4.0 points over SFT. In the project, RL's specific value is fixing what teacher forcing cannot: errors that compound when the model reads its own imperfect track, and the answer step ignoring the written track.
- Reward design for the project (my proposal, modelled on the cited ones): (1) answer correct, binary; (2) format valid; (3) SpaceR-style state reward, granted only when the answer is correct or independently: negative normalised distance between the written final position and the simulator's true position; (4) Video-R1-style contrastive bonus using twins: bonus only when both members of a twin pair are answered correctly, which removes reward for the last-frame shortcut that the earlier counterfactual-twin training fell into.
- Feasibility on one 24 GB GPU (estimate, not a reported measurement): published runs used 7B models with full or unspecified tuning on 4-8 80 GB GPUs. With a 3B model, LoRA, 64 tokens per frame (about 1K visual tokens for 16 frames) and short outputs, generating 8 rollouts per prompt sequentially and back-propagating with gradient accumulation should fit; the cost is wall-clock time (8 generations plus 8 backward passes per prompt). Output length is the main cost driver, so the terse track format helps. A few thousand prompts over several days is a realistic budget.
- Minimal experiment (RL): start from the best distilled checkpoint; 1-2K prompts, G=8, LoRA rank 16; compare reward (1)+(2) against the full shaped reward; evaluate twin-pair accuracy and written-position error; include an SFT-continued control with the same number of extra gradient steps so that the RL gain is not confounded with more training.

### Gaps
- VLM-R1 was not found by the search; no verified facts.
- No direct 24 GB memory measurement for GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-3B with video input, and no head-to-head sample-efficiency comparison of GRPO vs SFT at small data scale.
- ViGoRL base model sizes, compute and training recipe (warm start, RL algorithm) were not on the abstract page.
- No RL paper found that uses counterfactual twin pairs as the reward; T-GRPO's ordered-vs-shuffled contrast is the nearest.
- No PPO (as opposed to GRPO) result for VLM spatial reasoning was gathered.

## 9. Supervised fine-tuning choices that matter: what to unfreeze, auxiliary objectives, data scale, making the LLM use motion in visual tokens

### Takeaway
Published spatial fine-tunes keep the vision encoder frozen and train the connector plus the LLM (fully, or with high-rank LoRA such as 256), on 120K-590K samples; diagnostic work on video LLMs locates the loss of temporal information in the projector and in later LLM layers rather than in the encoder, which argues for training the merger/projector and using more LoRA capacity before touching the vision tower. The best-evidenced "auxiliary objective" is having the model write positions as text before answering.

### Cited Findings
- What is trained in the strongest spatial fine-tunes of Qwen2.5-VL-3B: Spatial-MLLM freezes both encoders and trains the connector and LLM backbone; GeoAlign freezes the Qwen ViT and VGGT and trains the fusion module and the language model (its plain fine-tune of Qwen2.5-VL-3B without geometry reaches 66.8 on VSI-Bench vs 30.6 zero-shot as reported by Spatial-MLLM). SpaceMind freezes both encoders, fully trains its fusion module and uses LoRA rank 256 (scaling 512) on the LLM. — [Spatial-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1), [GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1), [SpaceMind](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23075v2)
- Data scale used: Spatial-MLLM about 120K QA (40.0 for the plain 3B fine-tune); SpaceR 151K (7B, SFT 41.6); VLM-3R over 200K; GeoAlign about 460K, 1 epoch (plain 3B fine-tune 66.8); Cambrian-S 590,667 (3B: 57.3). — [Spatial-MLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23747v1), [SpaceR](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01805v2), [VLM-3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20279), [GeoAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12630v1), [Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- **Tracing the Arrow of Time** (Han et al., arXiv 8 May 2026): video-centric encoders with temporal modelling encode strong temporal signals while frame-centric encoders do not; the main bottleneck is transfer to the LLM, where performance often drops sharply; a Q-Former disrupts temporal information while a time-preserved MLP projector gives the LLM much better access; fixes proposed are a temporal-aware encoder, a time-preserved MLP projector and arrow-of-time supervision; 98.1% on AoT_PPB, up to +6.0 on VITATECS-Direction, +1.3 on TVBench. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07568)
- A probing study (arXiv 2602.11244, search summary only): temporal direction information is present in the visual representations but is progressively lost in later LLM layers, which it calls the more critical bottleneck. — [arXiv 2602.11244](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11244)
- Explicit temporal modules (search summaries only): a stackable temporal encoder placed before the projector works better than temporal modelling in semantic space with far fewer parameters (arXiv 2501.16786); TC-LLaVA freezes the vision encoder, trains projector and LLM, and adds temporal positional encoding and frame-wise attention masks inside the LLM (arXiv 2409.03206). — [arXiv 2501.16786](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16786v1), [TC-LLaVA](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.03206v1)
- Written state as the training target: SGCoT (tracks as text before the answer) 91% vs chance, while answer-only training of Qwen2.5-VL-3B stayed at chance for 60 epochs; SpaceR's written grid map of object positions is used as an RL reward term; VPD's summed, length-normalised answer and rationale losses. — [Shell Game](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08436), [SpaceR](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01805v2), [VPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03052v1)
- Self-generated continuous tokens with a decoder target as an auxiliary objective: CoVT (about 20 tokens reconstructing depth, segmentation, edges, DINO features; 3-16% gains on Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA). Auxiliary next-frame latent prediction head: Cambrian-S. — [CoVT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19418), [Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1)
- Cambrian-S uses a four-stage curriculum ending in spatial video instruction tuning; Video-R1 mixes image reasoning data into video training and uses a CoT SFT stage before RL. — [Cambrian-S](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04670v1), [Video-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21776v4)

### Inferences
- Unfreezing order to test, cheapest first (my proposal): (1) LoRA on attention only (current); (2) add LoRA on MLP layers and raise rank (64-256, following SpaceMind); (3) fully train the vision-to-LLM merger/projector (small parameter count; the diagnostic papers point at the projector); (4) LoRA on the last few vision-tower blocks. The project's own probe already reads floor positions from the frozen vision tokens, so (4) is the least likely to be needed; the bottleneck is the LLM reading what is there, which matches the published diagnosis.
- Note that Qwen2.5-VL's merger groups 2x2 patches (and pairs of frames) into one token; at 64 tokens per 224x224 frame, sub-token motion between adjacent frames may be averaged away before the LLM. Checking the probe on post-merger tokens for velocity (not just position) is a cheap diagnostic. The merger description is from background knowledge, unverified in this session.
- A position-regression head on LLM hidden states (as opposed to on vision tokens) is the diagnostic version of the earlier failed auxiliary loss: if positions become decodable from mid-layer LLM states at frame tokens but answers do not improve, the failure is in the answer path using the state, which the written-track target fixes by forcing the state through the text channel. I found no paper reporting such a head improving video QA by itself.
- Data scale: published 3B spatial fine-tunes use 120K-460K samples over broad task mixes; the project's task is far narrower and its simulator is unlimited, so 10-50K videos is a reasonable starting range (estimate). SGCoT's 300 samples is a lower bound that applied only because the base model could already track.
- Curriculum suggestion (my proposal, loosely following the staged schedules above): (i) write positions of visible objects per frame (pure perception, text output); (ii) add velocity and one-step extrapolation while the object is still visible, so the extrapolation can be checked against a later visible frame; (iii) extrapolate after exit; (iv) answer the question from the written state; then RL (section 8).
- Minimal experiment (fine-tuning choices): fix the written-track target and 10K videos, then run a small grid: {attention LoRA r16, attention+MLP LoRA r64, plus trainable merger}; measure written-position error per frame and final accuracy. Add one run with an LLM-hidden-state position head as a probe only (stop-gradient) to locate where position information is lost.
